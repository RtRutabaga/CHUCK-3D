# CHUCK — Game Bible

## High Concept

Chuck is a top-down action-adventure RPG inspired primarily by The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past, Link’s Awakening, EarthBound, and other 16-bit adventure games.

The game emphasizes exploration, environmental storytelling, memorable NPCs, light combat, subtle humor, and atmosphere.

Although the world eventually becomes cosmic in scope, the experience should always remain intimate and grounded.

The player is not trying to save the universe.

The player is simply following Chuck.

## Core Philosophy

The player should constantly feel curious, comfortable, slightly unsettled, occasionally amused, and increasingly convinced that something is wrong with the world.

The game should rarely explain itself directly. Players discover the truth through exploration. Environmental storytelling should always be preferred over exposition.

## Chuck

Chuck is the protagonist. The player always controls Chuck.

Chuck never speaks. Chuck never has internal monologue. The player is not projecting themselves onto Chuck. Chuck is his own character.

His personality is communicated entirely through animation, behavior, and how other characters react to him.

## Chuck’s Appearance

Chuck is approximately one foot tall, a gray rat, wears an oversized purple open jacket, frequently smokes cigarettes, has a permanent thousand-yard stare, and appears quietly exhausted.

His size is extremely important. The game world is built for humans. Everything should reinforce that Chuck is very small.

Tables feel enormous. Barrels are homes. Chickens are much larger than Chuck. Cats are dangerous. A missing plank becomes an uncrossable gap. A rope coil is the size of a room. Boots, buckets, mugs, and crates become meaningful level geometry.

Chuck’s size should constantly influence gameplay and visual design. It is never merely cosmetic.

## Chuck’s Personality

Chuck is quiet, patient, fearless, compassionate, observant, and persistent.

He is almost never surprised. He never panics. He rarely reacts dramatically.

The comedy comes from the contrast between Chuck’s calm demeanor and the absurd situations around him.

Chuck never behaves like a cartoon character.

The world is funny. Chuck is not.

## Who Chuck Is

Chuck is a fey spirit.

When characters in different D&D campaigns summoned Chuck, it was always the same Chuck appearing from the Astral Sea. When dismissed or “killed,” he returned there until summoned again.

Chuck has wandered between worlds for an unimaginably long time. He remembers places that no longer exist and people long forgotten.

Players slowly realize Chuck has lived through far more history than anyone around him.

## The Setting

The game begins in the Forgotten Realms, specifically Waterdeep.

The player should initially believe they are playing a fairly traditional fantasy adventure.

Only gradually do inconsistencies appear. Roads don’t make sense. Architecture changes. People remember contradictory histories. Entire landscapes begin blending together.

Eventually the player realizes this world contains fragments of many different realities.

This revelation should arrive naturally over many hours of play. Never announce it.

## The Astral Sea

The Astral Sea exists outside ordinary worlds. It resembles an endless, silent ocean beneath an infinite night sky. Broken fragments of realities drift through it.

Chuck naturally returns there between summons.

The Astral Sea should feel peaceful rather than frightening. Ancient. Quiet. Melancholy. Almost comforting.

## Story Structure

The story begins extremely small.

There is no prophecy. No chosen one. No narration explaining the universe.

The player simply begins controlling Chuck during an ordinary morning at Waterdeep Docks.

Only much later does the true scale of the story become apparent.

## Opening Area

The game begins at Waterdeep Docks.

Chuck’s home is Bobert’s barrel. Bobert is asleep inside. He never wakes and is never identified by name.

New players simply see a sleeping dockside vagrant. Players familiar with the D&D campaigns recognize him immediately.

Bobert is the only returning campaign character physically present in the opening area. He is never interacted with.

The opening map should be intentionally small: docks, warehouses, a small market, tavern, alleyways, and waterfront.

The player spends approximately ten minutes here learning movement and exploration.

## Exploration and Gameplay

Progression should rarely rely on magical barriers. Chuck’s small size naturally creates obstacles such as missing bridge planks, tall grass, cats, high tide, loose boards, heavy doors, and large gaps.

Gameplay follows classic top-down adventure design: exploration, environmental puzzles, light combat, item-based progression, NPC interaction, and hidden secrets.

Exploration should always be more important than combat.

## Sanity

Instead of health, Chuck has Sanity.

Damage lowers Sanity. Cigarettes restore it.

When Sanity reaches zero, Chuck quietly disappears. After a brief transition, he returns at the most recent Astral Anchor.

Death is treated as an accepted part of Chuck’s existence.

## NPC Writing

Dialogue should feel natural, deadpan, and grounded.

Nobody constantly tells jokes. Strange situations are treated as ordinary.

Example:

> “The lighthouse keeps ringing.”

There is no lighthouse. Nobody elaborates.

The humor comes from understatement.

## Tone

The emotional balance should be:

- 40% cozy exploration
- 25% melancholy
- 20% mystery
- 15% deadpan comedy

Never become cynical. Never become overly dark. The world should always feel worth saving.

## Environmental Storytelling

The environment tells the story.

Players should notice abandoned campsites, impossible architecture, forgotten roads, contradictory ruins, discarded cigarette butts, old paintings, and strange landmarks.

Small discoveries slowly build into larger revelations.

## Campaign References

Campaign references should exist primarily as environmental details: old posters, books, shrines, abandoned campsites, songs, and objects.

Most returning characters should not appear as active NPCs.

The world should feel like history, not a crossover event.

## Opening Area References

The opening area intentionally contains only one overt campaign reference besides Bobert:

> HEROD COVER BAND  
> TONIGHT ONLY

This references Bernard, who performs Herod’s old songs and is widely regarded as a Herod cover act rather than the original bard.

No additional campaign references should appear in the opening area.

The player should first experience Waterdeep as a believable Forgotten Realms location.

## Art Direction

Visual inspiration:

- A Link to the Past
- Link’s Awakening
- Chrono Trigger
- Final Fantasy VI

Warm color palette. Readable pixel art. Expressive but restrained animation. The world should feel hand-crafted.

Scale is one of the defining artistic principles. The player should constantly feel how small Chuck is relative to everything around him.

## Core Theme

Chuck is not extraordinary because he is powerful.

He is extraordinary because he continues walking.

He has witnessed worlds rise and fall. He has died countless times. He has been forgotten. He remembers those who have been forgotten by everyone else.

He keeps moving forward anyway.

That quiet persistence—not heroism, destiny, or power—is the emotional heart of the game.
